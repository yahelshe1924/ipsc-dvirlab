# Intact spinal cord stimulation: conclusions and next experiment

## Conclusions

- No reproducible stimulus-locked spike response was demonstrated in REC.
- Preparation 1: noise was comparable to baseline; one candidate event occurred ~70 ms after one 20 uA pulse, without repetition.
- Preparation 2: estimated filtered noise was 1.5-1.9 times baseline. Increased threshold crossings occurred both before and after stimulation, preventing attribution to an evoked response.
- Early responses remain unresolved because artifact and filtering exclusions mask the first milliseconds. Recovery estimates do not establish artifact-free recording.
- No stimulation threshold or optimal current was established. Neural activation cannot be ruled out.

## Next experiment

1. **Stabilize recording:** check electrode contact, impedance, reference/ground and noise. Document STIM/REC positions and current return.
2. **Extend baseline:** record 60-120 s before stimulation, add no-stimulation blocks between conditions and at the end, and repeat baseline after electrode changes.
3. **Increase repetitions:** pilot 30-50 pulses per condition after confirming stability. Monitor adaptation and drift; include independent preparations. These are planning targets, not a power calculation.
4. **Test configuration:** compare the current setup with one documented alternative, such as another REC position or bipolar stimulation if supported. Change one factor at a time.
5. **Scan current systematically:** keep pulse width fixed and remain within electrode-appropriate limits. Check impedance, compliance and charge per phase before increasing the range. Higher current alone is not justified by these results.
6. **Include controls:** characterize the artifact and use suitable technical and positive controls to verify recording capability and tissue viability.

**Success criterion:** a repeatable post-stimulus response exceeding matched pre-stimulus and baseline activity, with consistent timing and waveform, robust to reasonable thresholds and exclusion windows. For tract activation, also assess compound action potentials.

**Priority:** improve recording stability and early-response visibility, then increase baseline duration and trial counts.
